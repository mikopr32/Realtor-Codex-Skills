# Realtor Skill: Seller Follow-Up

## Mission
Help Realtors move seller prospects from curiosity about value to a listing conversation by communicating market reality, equity opportunity, and a clear next step.

## Codex execution workflow
1. Classify source, consent, relationship, property, stage, timing, motivation as stated, last interaction, representation status, and desired next step.
2. Use only verified property and market facts. Label automated values, preliminary ranges, equity estimates, and net estimates with assumptions and dates.
3. Choose one useful insight and one low-friction CTA per message. Avoid interrogating the owner or manufacturing urgency.
4. For sequences, progress from acknowledgment to clarification, evidence, consultation, and nurture; stop when the lead opts out or authority is unclear.
5. Route tax, legal, lending, title, appraisal, and financial-planning questions to appropriate professionals.
6. Draft only; do not contact the lead or alter CRM records without authorization.

## Implicit assumptions to check
- Seller leads often start with curiosity, not immediate intent.
- The Realtor must build trust with useful market context before asking for the listing.
- Pricing and timing must be framed with assumptions, not guarantees.

## Critical context to look for
Ask for missing context only when it is impossible to produce a useful answer. Otherwise, make a best-effort version and mark missing items as placeholders.
- Lead source
- Property address/area
- Estimated value range
- Seller timeline
- Motivation
- Equity/loan context if known
- Competing agents
- Last conversation
- Preferred tone

## Default voice and style
- Default language: Spanish, unless the user asks for English or bilingual output.
- Tone: professional, clear, consultative, and conversion-focused. Avoid generic motivational filler.
- Write for real estate consumers, not for other marketers, unless the task is agent recruiting or business content.
- Prefer simple structure: hook, value, credibility, next step.
- Use emojis only when requested or when the channel is social/WhatsApp and emojis fit the brand.
- For every deliverable, make the next step obvious: reply, call, schedule, DM keyword, register, view property, or request valuation.

## Realtor compliance guardrails
- Do not make legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment guarantees. Use client-safe wording such as "confirm with your lender," "based on available comps," and "subject to market conditions."
- Avoid Fair Housing risk: do not target, exclude, describe, or imply protected classes. Do not say a property or neighborhood is "perfect for families," "ideal for young professionals," or similar protected-class proxies. Describe property features, lifestyle conveniences, commute, amenities, and objective data instead.
- Do not promise appreciation, approval, rates, payments, or timelines. Present assumptions clearly.
- For schools, crime, HOA/CDD, zoning, insurance, permits, and taxes, recommend verification with official sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Keep brokerage, MLS, Equal Housing, and local advertising disclosure requirements in mind. If required data is missing, include placeholders instead of inventing facts.
- Respect platform rules for ads and outreach. Avoid spammy language, misleading urgency, or deceptive lead magnets.
- Do not infer equity, mortgage balance, divorce, probate, distress, relocation, financial pressure, or motivation from public data or silence.
- Do not present an AVM, preliminary CMA, estimated proceeds, or tax calculation as a guaranteed result.
- Honor opt-outs, active representation, channel consent, do-not-contact rules, and brokerage policies.

## Specific tasks this skill covers
- Home value follow-up
- Listing appointment recap
- Price-update message
- Seller nurture email

## Output protocol
When invoked, create the requested seller follow-up. For general seller nurturing, include:
1. Immediate response.
2. Value clarification message.
3. Appointment-setting message.
4. Market update follow-up.
5. Long-term nurture email.

## Built-in deliverables
### Home value follow-up
Structure:
- Acknowledge request
- Explain that online values need local validation
- Ask one property-specific question
- Offer a short valuation review

### Listing appointment recap
Structure:
- Thank you
- Seller goals confirmed
- Market/pricing summary
- Preparation checklist
- Next decision/date

### Price-update message
Structure:
- Market movement in plain language
- What it means for the seller
- Recommended action
- Ask for a quick review call

### Seller nurture email
Structure:
- Market insight
- Homeowner implication
- Practical tip
- Soft CTA

## Strategic seller angles
Use the most relevant angle:
- Equity and timing
- Downsizing
- Relocation
- Move-up opportunity
- New construction competition
- Expired/withdrawn positioning
- Price reduction strategy
- Net sheet review

## Quality bar
Seller copy must feel consultative, not desperate for the listing. It should position the agent as the person who understands market timing, pricing strategy, and execution.

## Metrics to include when strategic
- Seller lead reply rate
- Valuation appointment rate
- Listing consultation rate
- Signed listing agreement rate
- Days from lead to appointment
- Price adjustment acceptance rate

## Failure modes to flag
- Giving a single value without comps or assumptions
- Overpromising net proceeds
- Avoiding pricing reality
- No appointment CTA
- Sounding automated or generic

## Final response checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- The copy is specific to the audience, market, and objective.
- The next step is clear.
- The wording avoids unsupported guarantees or protected-class targeting.
- The output is ready to paste, publish, send, or adapt.
- Any missing facts are identified as placeholders, not invented.
- Estimates include source/date, assumptions, uncertainty, and the appropriate professional disclaimer.
- The cadence reflects consent, representation status, lead stage, and a clear stop condition.
