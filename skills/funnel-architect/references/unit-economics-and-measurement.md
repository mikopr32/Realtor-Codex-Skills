# Unit Economics and Measurement

## Backward model

Use:

- `sales = qualified appointments × show rate × close rate`
- `booked appointments = qualified appointments ÷ qualification rate`
- `leads = booked appointments ÷ lead-to-booked rate`
- `visits = leads ÷ page conversion rate`
- `spend = leads × CPL`
- `CAC = spend ÷ sales`
- `cost per appointment = spend ÷ shown appointments`

For real estate, model gross commission or contribution only from user-provided assumptions. Separate gross transaction value, gross commission income, split, fees, marketing cost and expected contribution.

## Scenarios

Produce low/base/high scenarios. Never use a benchmark as the user's forecast without labeling it. Show which variable has the greatest sensitivity.

## Capacity

Check:

- response capacity and speed-to-lead;
- appointment capacity;
- sales capacity;
- fulfillment or transaction capacity;
- CRM ownership;
- follow-up consistency.

Do not recommend lead volume the operator cannot service.

## Measurement hierarchy

1. Business outcome: revenue, contribution, closed transaction or retained customer.
2. Sales outcome: qualified appointment, show, proposal, contract.
3. Funnel outcome: lead, application, booking, attendance.
4. Experience: form completion, page conversion, reply and click.
5. Media: spend, impressions, reach, CPM, CTR and CPC.

Treat opens, views and engagement as diagnostic signals, not final success.

## Instrumentation

Define:

- source, medium, campaign and content UTMs;
- conversion events and deduplication;
- CRM source and lifecycle stage;
- consent source and timestamp;
- owner and speed-to-lead;
- lead-quality feedback;
- offline conversion import when supported;
- dashboard cadence and decision owner.

## Experiment contract

Every test needs:

- observation;
- hypothesis;
- single primary variable;
- primary KPI and guardrail metrics;
- minimum learning period or decision threshold;
- scale, iterate or stop rule;
- record of result and next action.

Avoid claiming statistical significance without sufficient data.
