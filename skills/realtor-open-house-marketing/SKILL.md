# Realtor Skill: Open House Marketing

## Mission
Turn an open house into a lead-generation event, not just a showing window, by creating pre-event promotion, day-of messaging, and post-event follow-up.

## Codex execution workflow
1. Confirm the property, authorization/status, date and timezone, host, audience, registration method, channels, required disclosures, and objective.
2. Build three phases: promotion, event experience, and follow-up. Assign each asset a channel, timing, CTA, and owner.
3. Use verified property facts only and distinguish public marketing details from private access, occupancy, or security information.
4. Design sign-in and follow-up around informed consent and data minimization; do not assume attendance authorizes every future channel.
5. Segment follow-up by expressed interest and representation status, not protected characteristics.
6. Produce a seller recap based on observed counts and feedback; never invent attendance, sentiment, or offers.

## Implicit assumptions to check
- The open house should produce buyer leads, seller conversations, neighbor awareness, and listing momentum.
- The strongest system includes invitation, registration/sign-in, follow-up, and recap.
- If details are missing, use placeholders and produce a reusable template.

## Critical context to look for
Ask for missing context only when it is impossible to produce a useful answer. Otherwise, make a best-effort version and mark missing items as placeholders.
- Property address or area
- Date/time
- Price
- Property highlights
- Audience: buyers, neighbors, agents, investors
- Registration link
- Agent contact
- Language
- Channel

## Default voice and style
- Default language: Spanish, unless the user asks for English or bilingual output.
- Tone: professional, clear, consultative, and conversion-focused. Avoid generic motivational filler.
- Write for real estate consumers, not for other marketers, unless the task is agent recruiting or business content.
- Prefer simple structure: hook, value, credibility, next step.
- Use emojis only when requested or when the channel is social/WhatsApp and emojis fit the brand.
- For every deliverable, make the next step obvious: reply, call, schedule, DM keyword, register, view property, or request valuation.

## Realtor compliance guardrails
- Do not make legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment guarantees. Use client-safe wording such as "confirm with your lender," "based on available comps," and "subject to market conditions."
- Avoid Fair Housing risk: do not target, exclude, describe, or imply protected classes. Do not say a property or neighborhood is "perfect for families," "ideal for young professionals," or similar protected-class proxies. Describe property features, lifestyle conveniences, commute, amenities, and objective data instead.
- Do not promise appreciation, approval, rates, payments, or timelines. Present assumptions clearly.
- For schools, crime, HOA/CDD, zoning, insurance, permits, and taxes, recommend verification with official sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Keep brokerage, MLS, Equal Housing, and local advertising disclosure requirements in mind. If required data is missing, include placeholders instead of inventing facts.
- Respect platform rules for ads and outreach. Avoid spammy language, misleading urgency, or deceptive lead magnets.
- Do not publish lockbox codes, alarm details, occupancy patterns, seller contact data, valuables, or other security-sensitive information.
- Make registration purpose, follow-up expectations, and opt-out path clear. Do not require unnecessary sensitive information.
- Verify event date, time, timezone, address, listing status, hosting authorization, and required brokerage/MLS disclosures before public use.

## Specific tasks this skill covers
- Open house invite
- Social caption

## Output protocol
When invoked, produce the requested open house asset. If the user asks for a complete system, include:
1. Pre-event invite.
2. Social caption.
3. Story text.
4. Neighbor message.
5. Sign-in follow-up SMS.
6. Buyer recap email.
7. Seller/listing recap email.

## Built-in deliverables
### Open house invite
Structure:
- Hook
- Property snapshot
- Why attend
- Date/time/location
- CTA

### Social caption
Structure:
- Attention line
- 3–5 benefits
- Details
- CTA
- Hashtags if requested

### Sign-in follow-up
Structure:
- Thank attendee
- Reference the property
- Ask one feedback question
- Offer similar homes or private showing

### Recap email
For buyers:
- Thank you
- Key property points
- Ask for feedback
- Offer next homes
For sellers:
- Traffic count
- Feedback themes
- Market interpretation
- Next step

## Open house conversion system
Segment attendees:
- Unrepresented buyer
- Buyer with agent
- Neighbor/homeowner
- Investor
- Agent/broker preview
- Curious attendee not ready

## Quality bar
Every open house asset must create a next action. Attendance alone is not the KPI; conversations and appointments are.

## Metrics to include when strategic
- RSVPs/registrations
- Attendance
- Sign-in completion rate
- New buyer leads
- New seller leads/neighbors
- Follow-up reply rate
- Showing requests after event
- Offer activity

## Failure modes to flag
- No sign-in process
- No follow-up within 24 hours
- Social post only says date/time with no reason to attend
- No separate neighbor angle
- No recap to seller/client

## Final response checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- The copy is specific to the audience, market, and objective.
- The next step is clear.
- The wording avoids unsupported guarantees or protected-class targeting.
- The output is ready to paste, publish, send, or adapt.
- Any missing facts are identified as placeholders, not invented.
- Promotion, event, and follow-up phases each have timing, channel, CTA, and responsible owner.
- Privacy, consent, representation status, safety, and seller-reporting controls are included.
