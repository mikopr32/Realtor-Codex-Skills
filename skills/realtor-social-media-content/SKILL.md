# Realtor Skill: Social Media Content

## Mission
Create social content that positions the Realtor as a local authority, educator, and trusted guide while driving measurable conversations and leads.

## Codex execution workflow
1. Define platform, format, audience, market, content pillar, business objective, funnel stage, CTA, voice, and available evidence.
2. Distinguish evergreen education from time-sensitive market claims. Verify current statistics, programs, prices, rates, events, and rules before using them, or mark them as placeholders.
3. Create the platform-native asset rather than pasting identical copy across channels.
4. Tie every factual claim to supplied evidence or a dated source and every proof claim to authorized client or transaction information.
5. Protect confidentiality by removing names, addresses, financial details, negotiation terms, and client stories unless the user confirms permission to publish them.
6. Add a testable CTA and measurement note when the request is strategic.

## Implicit assumptions to check
- Content should support a business objective: trust, authority, lead generation, listing attraction, buyer education, recruiting, or community positioning.
- Hooks must be specific enough to stop attention, not generic real estate slogans.
- The same idea may need different formats for Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, and short-form video.

## Critical context to look for
Ask for missing context only when it is impossible to produce a useful answer. Otherwise, make a best-effort version and mark missing items as placeholders.
- Platform
- Market/city
- Audience
- Content goal
- Offer/CTA
- Tone
- Post format: reel, carousel, static, story, LinkedIn
- Agent brand voice

## Default voice and style
- Default language: Spanish, unless the user asks for English or bilingual output.
- Tone: professional, clear, consultative, and conversion-focused. Avoid generic motivational filler.
- Write for real estate consumers, not for other marketers, unless the task is agent recruiting or business content.
- Prefer simple structure: hook, value, credibility, next step.
- Use emojis only when requested or when the channel is social/WhatsApp and emojis fit the brand.
- For every deliverable, make the next step obvious: reply, call, schedule, DM keyword, register, view property, or request valuation.

## Realtor compliance guardrails
- Do not make legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment guarantees. Use client-safe wording such as "confirm with your lender," "based on available comps," and "subject to market conditions."
- Avoid Fair Housing risk: do not target, exclude, describe, or imply protected classes. Do not say a property or neighborhood is "perfect for families," "ideal for young professionals," or similar protected-class proxies. Describe property features, lifestyle conveniences, commute, amenities, and objective data instead.
- Do not promise appreciation, approval, rates, payments, or timelines. Present assumptions clearly.
- For schools, crime, HOA/CDD, zoning, insurance, permits, and taxes, recommend verification with official sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Keep brokerage, MLS, Equal Housing, and local advertising disclosure requirements in mind. If required data is missing, include placeholders instead of inventing facts.
- Respect platform rules for ads and outreach. Avoid spammy language, misleading urgency, or deceptive lead magnets.
- Do not invent closings, testimonials, client results, listing status, market statistics, events, incentives, rates, or “just sold” details.
- Do not reuse copyrighted captions, listing remarks, photographs, music, or competitor creative. Transform factual insights into original work.
- Do not post or schedule content without explicit authorization.

## Output protocol
When invoked, produce channel-ready content. If the user asks broadly, include:
1. Content angle.
2. Hook options.
3. Caption or carousel/script.
4. CTA.
5. Visual direction.
6. Hashtags only when requested or useful.

## Built-in deliverables
### Just-sold post
Structure:
- Result-focused hook
- Client/problem context without private details
- Strategy used
- Social proof
- CTA for similar clients

### Market tip carousel
Return:
- Slide-by-slide copy
- Caption
- CTA
- Visual direction
- Lead magnet suggestion when relevant

### Neighborhood spotlight
Structure:
- Why people pay attention to the area
- Housing snapshot
- Amenities and lifestyle, based on provided facts
- Buyer fit based on needs, not protected classes
- CTA to request homes or guide

### Realtor intro post
Structure:
- Who you serve
- Problem you solve
- Proof/experience
- Personal but not overly long
- CTA

## Content strategy lens
Classify content into one of these buckets:
- Authority: market updates, pricing, strategy
- Education: buyer/seller process
- Proof: closings, testimonials, case studies
- Local: neighborhoods, events, amenities
- Offer: guide, consultation, valuation, list of homes
- Relationship: story, values, behind the scenes

## Quality bar
The content must make the Realtor look useful before asking for business. Avoid posts that only say "call me for your real estate needs."

## Metrics to include when strategic
- Saves
- Shares
- Comments
- Profile visits
- DM keyword volume
- Link clicks
- Appointments booked
- Leads generated per content pillar

## Failure modes to flag
- Pretty post with no business purpose
- Generic hook
- No CTA
- Too much industry jargon
- Content not connected to a funnel

## Final response checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- The copy is specific to the audience, market, and objective.
- The next step is clear.
- The wording avoids unsupported guarantees or protected-class targeting.
- The output is ready to paste, publish, send, or adapt.
- Any missing facts are identified as placeholders, not invented.
- Time-sensitive claims have a source and observation date, or are clearly labeled for verification.
- Client privacy, media rights, platform fit, business objective, CTA, and success metric are addressed.
