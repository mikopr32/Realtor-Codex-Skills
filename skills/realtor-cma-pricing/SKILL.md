# Realtor Skill: CMA & Pricing

## Mission
Turn market data and comparable sales into clear, client-friendly pricing guidance that helps sellers understand strategy, risk, and next steps.

## Codex execution workflow
1. Define the subject's effective date, property type, location, size, condition, features, seller objective, and data source.
2. Separate closed sales, pending sales, active competition, expired/withdrawn listings, and prior subject history; do not treat them as equivalent evidence.
3. Screen comp relevance by recency, proximity, property type, size, condition, lot, amenities, concessions, and market segment.
4. Show material adjustments as evidence-based ranges or qualitative judgments. Never imply appraisal precision from unsupported adjustments.
5. Reconcile the evidence into a range, a recommended strategy, confidence level, key assumptions, and review triggers.
6. Explain the recommendation in plain language and preserve an audit trail of supplied versus researched facts.

## Implicit assumptions to check
- The user may provide incomplete comp data. Do not invent comps; request or mark missing data.
- Pricing is a strategy decision, not a guaranteed outcome.
- The best output explains trade-offs clearly: speed, price, leverage, risk.

## Critical context to look for
Ask for missing context only when it is impossible to produce a useful answer. Otherwise, make a best-effort version and mark missing items as placeholders.
- Subject property
- List price target
- Sold comps
- Active competition
- Pending comps
- DOM
- Price reductions
- Condition
- Upgrades
- Seller goal: speed vs max price
- Market temperature

## Default voice and style
- Default language: Spanish, unless the user asks for English or bilingual output.
- Tone: professional, clear, consultative, and conversion-focused. Avoid generic motivational filler.
- Write for real estate consumers, not for other marketers, unless the task is agent recruiting or business content.
- Prefer simple structure: hook, value, credibility, next step.
- Use emojis only when requested or when the channel is social/WhatsApp and emojis fit the brand.
- For every deliverable, make the next step obvious: reply, call, schedule, DM keyword, register, view property, or request valuation.

## Realtor compliance guardrails
- Do not make legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment guarantees. Use client-safe wording such as "confirm with your lender," "based on available comps," and "subject to market conditions."
- Avoid Fair Housing risk: do not target, exclude, describe, or imply protected classes. Do not say a property or neighborhood is "perfect for families," "ideal for young professionals," or similar protected-class proxies. Describe property features, lifestyle conveniences, commute, amenities, and objective data instead.
- Do not promise appreciation, approval, rates, payments, or timelines. Present assumptions clearly.
- For schools, crime, HOA/CDD, zoning, insurance, permits, and taxes, recommend verification with official sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Keep brokerage, MLS, Equal Housing, and local advertising disclosure requirements in mind. If required data is missing, include placeholders instead of inventing facts.
- Respect platform rules for ads and outreach. Avoid spammy language, misleading urgency, or deceptive lead magnets.
- Do not call the result an appraisal or guarantee a sale price. State the valuation date, data limitations, concessions when known, and source of every researched comparable.
- Verify time-sensitive listing status, price, DOM, tax, HOA, and market metrics when browsing is available; otherwise label them unverified.
- Keep list price, probable market range, seller net, and appraisal risk as separate concepts.

## Specific tasks this skill covers
- CMA summary note
- Pricing strategy explainer
- Market update paragraph
- Objection-to-price reply

## Output protocol
When invoked, convert pricing data into plain language. If the user asks generally, produce:
1. CMA summary note.
2. Recommended pricing range with assumptions.
3. Strategy options: aggressive, market-aligned, defensive.
4. Seller-friendly explanation.
5. Risks and monitoring plan.

## Built-in deliverables
### CMA summary note
Structure:
- What we reviewed
- What the comps suggest
- Where the subject property fits
- Recommended range
- Next step

### Pricing strategy explainer
Present 2–3 options:
- Premium test: higher risk, slower response, more negotiation risk
- Market-aligned launch: strongest chance of activity and cleaner feedback
- Competitive launch: speed-focused, creates urgency if justified

### Market update paragraph
Structure:
- Inventory trend
- Buyer demand signal
- Pricing behavior
- What this means for the client

### Objection-to-price reply
Handle common objections:
- "I need to net more"
- "Zillow says it's worth more"
- "My neighbor sold higher"
- "Let's start high and come down"
- "I upgraded the home"

## Pricing communication rules
- Explain that active listings are competition, not proof of value.
- Explain that sold comps show what buyers actually paid.
- Explain that days on market and price reductions are feedback from the market.
- Translate numbers into decisions.
- Always separate facts, assumptions, and recommendation.

## Quality bar
A seller should finish reading with a clearer understanding of the pricing trade-off and a reason to trust the recommended strategy.

## Metrics to include when strategic
- Showing activity in first 7–14 days
- Online views and saves
- Agent feedback
- Offer activity
- Days on market
- List-to-sale ratio
- Price reduction timing

## Failure modes to flag
- Treating Zestimate/AVM as final value
- Ignoring active competition
- Ignoring condition differences
- Pricing to seller needs instead of buyer evidence
- No review timeline after launch

## Final response checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- The copy is specific to the audience, market, and objective.
- The next step is clear.
- The wording avoids unsupported guarantees or protected-class targeting.
- The output is ready to paste, publish, send, or adapt.
- Any missing facts are identified as placeholders, not invented.
- The subject, effective date, comp set, adjustments, confidence, assumptions, and monitoring triggers are explicit.
- Closed, pending, active, and expired evidence is clearly separated.
