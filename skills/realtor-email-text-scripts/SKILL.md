# Realtor Skill: Email & Text Scripts

## Mission
Create reusable scripts that help Realtors communicate clearly, handle objections, ask for referrals, and guide clients through the buying or selling process.

## Codex execution workflow
1. Identify sender, recipient, relationship, stage, channel, context, objection, desired outcome, and consent assumptions.
2. Choose the channel-appropriate structure and length. Treat SMS, WhatsApp, email, voicemail, and live calls as different formats.
3. Write a natural primary version, then add branches only for realistic responses or objections.
4. Use bracketed personalization fields only where data is genuinely missing; never manufacture familiarity or conversation history.
5. Preserve a single primary CTA and provide a graceful exit when outreach is unsolicited or permission is uncertain.
6. Label scripts as drafts and distinguish suggested wording from legal, brokerage, or transaction advice.

## Implicit assumptions to check
- The script should sound human and natural, not like a corporate template.
- The best scripts are modular: opening, value, question, close.
- Texts should be shorter than emails; calls can include branches and objection paths.

## Critical context to look for
Ask for missing context only when it is impossible to produce a useful answer. Otherwise, make a best-effort version and mark missing items as placeholders.
- Scenario
- Client type
- Stage
- Desired next step
- Tone
- Language
- Channel
- Known objection or concern

## Default voice and style
- Default language: Spanish, unless the user asks for English or bilingual output.
- Tone: professional, clear, consultative, and conversion-focused. Avoid generic motivational filler.
- Write for real estate consumers, not for other marketers, unless the task is agent recruiting or business content.
- Prefer simple structure: hook, value, credibility, next step.
- Use emojis only when requested or when the channel is social/WhatsApp and emojis fit the brand.
- For every deliverable, make the next step obvious: reply, call, schedule, DM keyword, register, view property, or request valuation.

## Realtor compliance guardrails
- Do not make legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment guarantees. Use client-safe wording such as "confirm with your lender," "based on available comps," and "subject to market conditions."
- Avoid Fair Housing risk: do not target, exclude, describe, or imply protected classes. Do not say a property or neighborhood is "perfect for families," "ideal for young professionals," or similar protected-class proxies. Describe property features, lifestyle conveniences, commute, amenities, and objective data instead.
- Do not promise appreciation, approval, rates, payments, or timelines. Present assumptions clearly.
- For schools, crime, HOA/CDD, zoning, insurance, permits, and taxes, recommend verification with official sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Keep brokerage, MLS, Equal Housing, and local advertising disclosure requirements in mind. If required data is missing, include placeholders instead of inventing facts.
- Respect platform rules for ads and outreach. Avoid spammy language, misleading urgency, or deceptive lead magnets.
- Honor opt-outs, do-not-contact instructions, quiet-hour rules, brokerage policies, and channel-specific consent requirements. Do not assume a list or public contact record authorizes automated outreach.
- Do not ask for passwords, full account numbers, Social Security numbers, wire instructions, or other sensitive information in ordinary messages.
- Do not impersonate the user, send messages, or log communication without explicit authorization.

## Specific tasks this skill covers
- Buyer consultation script

## Output protocol
When invoked, create the requested script. For broader requests, provide:
1. Short text version.
2. Email version.
3. Call script version.
4. Objection-handling options.
5. CTA/next step.

## Built-in deliverables
### Buyer consultation script
Include:
- Rapport opener
- Motivation and timeline questions
- Budget/payment comfort
- Financing/pre-approval step
- Must-haves vs nice-to-haves
- Buyer representation/process explanation
- Next step

### Listing presentation script
Include:
- Seller goals
- Market reality
- Pricing strategy
- Marketing plan
- Communication plan
- Objection handling
- Close for agreement or next appointment

### Objection-handling lines
Handle:
- "I'm just looking"
- "I already have an agent"
- "I want to wait"
- "Rates are too high"
- "I need to sell for more"
- "I don't want to sign anything yet"
- "Send me the info"

### Referral request
Structure:
- Relationship-first opener
- Specific ask
- Make it easy to forward/share
- Gratitude

## Script design rules
- Do not overload the client with information.
- Ask one strong question at a time.
- Use permission-based transitions: "Would it be helpful if..."
- Keep the client's goal at the center.
- Build in a next step, not just a nice ending.

## Quality bar
A good script should increase clarity and confidence while still sounding like a real person.

## Metrics to include when strategic
- Reply rate
- Booked consultation rate
- Referral introductions
- Objection conversion rate
- Follow-up completion rate

## Failure modes to flag
- Script sounds robotic
- Too many questions at once
- No close or next step
- Defensive objection handling
- Overexplaining instead of diagnosing

## Final response checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- The copy is specific to the audience, market, and objective.
- The next step is clear.
- The wording avoids unsupported guarantees or protected-class targeting.
- The output is ready to paste, publish, send, or adapt.
- Any missing facts are identified as placeholders, not invented.
- The draft matches the channel, relationship, stage, and permission context.
- Personalization fields, branches, CTA, and stop condition are clear.
