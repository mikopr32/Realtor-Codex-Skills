# SEO, AEO and geographic editorial protocol

Use this protocol for blogs, newsletters and long-form market articles. Optimize for usefulness and factual retrieval, not keyword density.

## Search intent

Define one primary intent: informational, comparison, local decision support or transaction preparation. Record primary question, reader, geography, funnel stage and desired next action.

Do not combine unrelated intents in one page. Use one canonical topic and 3–6 supporting questions.

## AEO answer block

Place a direct 40–60 word answer after the title when the topic supports a concise answer. Include the applicable geography, period and qualification. Do not force certainty when the evidence is mixed.

Make headings answer real questions. Use short definitions, lists or tables only when they improve extraction and comprehension.

## Geographic relevance

- Match each location mention to supporting evidence or a genuine reader action.
- Distinguish Florida, region, metro, county, city, ZIP, community and building.
- Do not insert Miami, Orlando, Tampa or West Palm Beach merely to rank.
- For multi-market comparisons, use the same metric, period and definition.
- Include locally authoritative entities and source links where relevant.

## On-page package

Provide when requested or useful:

- Primary query and search intent.
- H1 plus H2/H3 outline.
- Meta title around 50–60 characters when practical.
- Meta description around 140–160 characters when practical.
- Short readable slug.
- Excerpt.
- Internal-link opportunities described by topic, not invented URLs.
- External citations to authoritative sources.
- Image concept and factual alt text.
- Suggested schema type such as Article, NewsArticle or FAQPage.

Do not output FAQ schema when the visible page will not contain the same questions and answers. Treat character guidance as editorial targets, not guaranteed search-engine display limits.

## Article structure

1. Accurate local headline.
2. AEO answer block.
3. Key takeaways.
4. What changed and when.
5. Evidence and source definitions.
6. Local impact by relevant audience.
7. Tradeoffs, mixed signals and limitations.
8. Practical next steps.
9. CTA.
10. Sources and last-reviewed date.

## Newsletter adaptation

Use a subject line, preview text, personal opener, concise news summary, “what this means,” practical action, CTA and source note. Avoid pasting a full blog unchanged into email.

## Quality checks

- Search intent is answered near the top.
- Every number has geography, period and source.
- Keyword use is natural.
- Location mentions are evidence-based.
- Headline and metadata do not overstate the story.
- The answer block matches the article body.
- Legal/regulatory language separates source text from interpretation.
- The CTA points to an available next step.
