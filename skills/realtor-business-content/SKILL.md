# Realtor Skill: Realtor Business Content

## Mission
Create professional business copy that strengthens the Realtor's positioning, credibility, referrals, client retention, and brand authority.

## Codex execution workflow
1. Identify audience, channel, objective, desired action, voice, market, niche, and available proof.
2. Build a concise message hierarchy: audience, problem, differentiator, evidence, service promise, and CTA.
3. Preserve the user's facts and voice. Mark missing names, credentials, production numbers, designations, affiliations, awards, and testimonials as placeholders.
4. Adapt length and structure to the actual channel instead of recycling one generic paragraph.
5. Deliver the requested asset plus a shorter reusable version when it materially helps.
6. Check every factual or reputation claim before finalizing.

## Implicit assumptions to check
- The copy should position the Realtor strategically, not just describe credentials.
- Proof, niche, market, client type, and service promise matter more than vague claims.
- If the user provides a testimonial, preserve meaning and do not invent client claims.

## Critical context to look for
Ask for missing context only when it is impossible to produce a useful answer. Otherwise, make a best-effort version and mark missing items as placeholders.
- Agent name
- Market
- Brokerage
- Niche
- Years of experience
- Production/proof
- Audience
- Brand tone
- Language
- Channel

## Default voice and style
- Default language: Spanish, unless the user asks for English or bilingual output.
- Tone: professional, clear, consultative, and conversion-focused. Avoid generic motivational filler.
- Write for real estate consumers, not for other marketers, unless the task is agent recruiting or business content.
- Prefer simple structure: hook, value, credibility, next step.
- Use emojis only when requested or when the channel is social/WhatsApp and emojis fit the brand.
- For every deliverable, make the next step obvious: reply, call, schedule, DM keyword, register, view property, or request valuation.

## Realtor compliance guardrails
- Do not make legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment guarantees. Use client-safe wording such as "confirm with your lender," "based on available comps," and "subject to market conditions."
- Avoid Fair Housing risk: do not target, exclude, describe, or imply protected classes. Do not say a property or neighborhood is "perfect for families," "ideal for young professionals," or similar protected-class proxies. Describe property features, lifestyle conveniences, commute, amenities, and objective data instead.
- Do not promise appreciation, approval, rates, payments, or timelines. Present assumptions clearly.
- For schools, crime, HOA/CDD, zoning, insurance, permits, and taxes, recommend verification with official sources instead of making unsupported claims.
- Keep brokerage, MLS, Equal Housing, and local advertising disclosure requirements in mind. If required data is missing, include placeholders instead of inventing facts.
- Respect platform rules for ads and outreach. Avoid spammy language, misleading urgency, or deceptive lead magnets.
- Never invent awards, rankings, sales volume, years of experience, designations, brokerage affiliations, media mentions, client quotes, or review language.
- When rewriting a testimonial, preserve its meaning and identify substantive edits; do not convert an agent-authored claim into a client quote.
- Do not publish, post, email, or request reviews on the user's behalf without explicit authorization.

## Specific tasks this skill covers
- Agent bio

## Output protocol
When invoked, produce the requested business asset. If the user asks broadly, provide:
1. Strategic positioning statement.
2. Main copy.
3. Short version.
4. CTA.
5. Optional bilingual version if useful.

## Built-in deliverables
### Agent bio
Structure:
- Who the agent serves
- Market and specialty
- Proof/experience
- Service philosophy
- Personal touch if provided
- CTA
Create versions for:
- Instagram bio
- Website bio
- LinkedIn bio
- Speaker/event bio

### Monthly newsletter
Structure:
- Personal opener
- Market insight
- Practical buyer/seller tip
- Local/community note
- CTA
- Referral reminder when appropriate

### Client review request
Structure:
- Thank you
- Specific reason their review matters
- Simple instructions/link placeholder
- Optional prompt questions to make it easy

### Testimonial rewrite
Rules:
- Preserve truth and tone.
- Correct grammar and flow.
- Do not add claims the client did not make.
- Create short, medium, and social proof versions when useful.

## Positioning framework
Use this when building brand copy:
- Audience: who the Realtor helps
- Problem: what pain/confusion they solve
- Mechanism: how they guide the client differently
- Proof: results, experience, designations, client stories
- Promise: clear, believable value
- CTA: next step

## Quality bar
Business copy should make the agent sound specific, credible, and referable. Avoid generic claims like "dedicated to excellent service" unless supported by a real differentiator.

## Metrics to include when strategic
- Profile conversion rate
- Newsletter open/reply rate
- Referral requests sent
- Reviews collected
- Website contact conversions
- Speaking/event inquiries

## Failure modes to flag
- Bio sounds like every other Realtor
- No niche or audience
- Too much credential list, not enough client value
- Testimonial rewrite changes meaning
- No CTA

## Final response checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- The copy is specific to the audience, market, and objective.
- The next step is clear.
- The wording avoids unsupported guarantees or protected-class targeting.
- The output is ready to paste, publish, send, or adapt.
- Any missing facts are identified as placeholders, not invented.
- Each credibility claim is supported by supplied or verified evidence.
- The asset fits the requested channel, length, audience, and brand voice.
